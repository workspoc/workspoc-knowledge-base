# Understanding Pillars, Domains & Practices

Pillars, Domains and Practices turn broad strategic priorities into consistent, measurable areas.

## Why this matters

A shared hierarchy connects question design to analysis. Teams can see a high-level outcome, explore its contributing areas and identify a specific capability to improve.

## How it works

- **Pillar** — a high-level strategic area the organisation wants to measure.
- **Domain** — a measurable area contained within a Pillar.
- **Practice** — a specific capability or behaviour within a Domain.

> **Example hierarchy**  
> **Pillar:** Customer Experience → **Domain:** Customer Engagement → **Practice:** Proactive Communication

Questions and survey insights can be linked back to these levels, supporting both executive summaries and detailed diagnosis.

## Step-by-step

1. Agree a small set of strategic Pillars.
2. Define distinct Domains under each Pillar.
3. Add observable, actionable Practices under each Domain.
4. Write plain-language descriptions and assign ownership.
5. Map validated questions to the most relevant level.
6. Test whether results will lead to a clear conversation or action.

## Best practices

- Keep levels mutually clear and collectively useful.
- Define Practices as capabilities teams can influence.
- Avoid duplicate concepts under different labels.
- Govern framework changes between survey cycles.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can one question relate to more than one area?** It may, but choose mappings carefully to avoid double-counting or ambiguous analysis.

**How many levels are required?** Use enough detail to diagnose performance without creating an unwieldy framework.
